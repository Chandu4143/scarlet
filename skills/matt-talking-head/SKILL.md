# Matt’s talking-head edit

Use this guide for my presenter footage. A bare `/matt-talking-head` covers the current edit, including camera punch-ins and any screen demos. If I name a pass, source, selection or cut, work just on that. Keep an existing edit’s sync and composition. For separate raw camera, screen and mic recordings, align the common sound, play the dialogue mic once, and carry cuts across all synchronized tracks. Check that my mouth, voice and the action I describe still line up.

## Choose the take

Keep the complete delivery meant for the viewer. Read through the restart and into what comes next. A second attempt may correct a number, add a qualification or finish a sentence the first attempt abandoned. Keep those details, deliberate emphasis and genuine reactions. If the wording is equivalent but the splice changes which performance survives, listen for the take I meant. Read the whole joined sentence and check the edited audio for a doubled word, the tail of a false start or a clipped syllable. Recheck the join after moving an edge.

Cut anything that sounds like direction to my editor, including likely asides, their lead-in and the wait around them. “Use the second take” is a direction. “Let me show you the second result” is for the viewer. Apply the removal and give **each** such cut a `check` naming the direction. Quote my words in its edit summary. Carry out directions that belong to the requested pass and say what you did. Put other requests, such as an opening montage or a music cue, in the summary for me. Where an aside runs into narration, cut the aside alone and flag the join. If Scarlet holds the cut, tell me what remains to review.

## Pace the dialogue

Judge the quiet on both sides of a join as one audible beat. Within a sentence, take out the wait and let the words run together naturally. Between sentences, about a quarter second is a starting point. Half a second of empty waiting usually drags. Give a reveal, reaction, purposeful breath or change of subject the time it earns. Trim fillers, coughs, lip smacks, chimes and off-mic interruptions when the line reads cleanly without them. Preserve hesitation that carries meaning and quiet speech the viewer needs. A transcript gap can contain speech or a demonstration. Check the sound and picture before trimming it.

Cut silent typing, searching and loading to the result. Hold the result long enough to see what changed. Flag any silence of two seconds or more that you keep in full, with what the viewer sees or hears during it.

Use a **two-frame audio crossfade** at dialogue joins and a straight picture cut. Bring my voice in promptly when the picture cuts back to me, especially in the first 40 seconds. On a straight pickup, around a tenth of a second between the picture cut and my first word is a good starting point. Later, a completed thought can breathe for closer to an eighth of a second. Keep a longer beat for a demonstration, a reaction or a purposeful breath. If my voice already enters naturally, keep that timing.

Measure from the incoming picture to the first word with `timeline_join_audit`. Choose the nearest whole frame that protects the first consonant and the release of the outgoing word through the crossfade. Trust the recording when the transcript disagrees. Use `dialogue_check_repeats` on the edited dialogue and check the surrounding delivery before removing a repeat. Apply confident cuts. Stage a genuine choice for me with the words or reaction I would lose.

## Show the demo

During a synchronized screen demo, keep my camera inset at a consistent size in the bottom right. Frame the moving recording around what I am showing. Fill the picture with the game or preview pane when I play it, the passage when I read it, or the chart and its labels when I discuss a result. Go wide enough to show both sides of a comparison or keep a tall demo intact. Keep controls I use and words I read clear of the inset.

Follow the narration. Ease into the subject as I mention it, settling in about a second. Hold through small pointer movements. Ease back out or across when the subject changes. When the narration moves quickly between related details, one wider frame can cover them. Check frames through the move and at the reveal for the label, control or result the viewer needs to see.

## Camera punch-ins

On full-screen camera footage, cut from wide to head and shoulders across a jump cut or on a line that needs emphasis. About 115–130% is a starting point when the source stays sharp. Hold for a few seconds, follow my actual face position, and keep my eyes and mouth comfortably framed. Return wide at the end of the thought. Keep the demo inset steady.

## When requested

- **Captions.** Caption the retained speech in the project style. Correct misheard names, products and numbers. Break at spoken phrases, keeping names and units together, on one or two readable lines. Check them over the picture so the viewer can still see my face and the demo.
- **Slide B-roll.** Time each supplied slide or requested reveal to the words that introduce it. For new slide art, use one visual idea, a real screenshot, portrait, logo or headline where it helps, large type and few words. Keep the upper right open in the artwork. On a reveal, hold the setup in place and add only the new element. Put spoken detail and sourcing in the slide notes and check the rendered slide at video size.
